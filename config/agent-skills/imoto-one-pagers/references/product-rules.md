# Product Claim Rules

## Driver Shield

Use:

- Product/pilot language.
- Owned hardware, operator-owned/local data, no cab-facing camera, modular outside coverage.
- AI-enriched evidence that makes footage easier to search, review, explain, and share.
- Evidence visuals: four-camera views, evidence list, incident review, map, telemetry, extracted event context.
- CTA: talk through a recent disputed incident or scope a pilot install.

Avoid:

- Guaranteed claim wins, lower insurance premiums, fixed public pricing, proven dispute reduction, fixed detection accuracy, battery/runtime guarantees, IP-rating promises, accident prevention, real-time AI monitoring, or enterprise/fleet-grade overclaiming.

## Route Optimization

Use:

- Demo/audit language.
- Comparing current plans, constraints, distance, time, fuel, estimated cost, and workflow friction.
- Multi-route map screenshots plus a clear audit-breakdown visual when available.
- CTA: send one week of route/job/vehicle/depot/constraint exports.

Avoid:

- Guaranteed fuel savings, synthetic demo savings as customer proof, replacing the TMS, fully autonomous dispatch, real-time dynamic routing unless built for that customer, or "AI route optimization" as the main unsupported claim.

## Predictive Maintenance

Use:

- Fleet health review and data-readiness language.
- Visuals showing component health, alert triage, maintenance record readiness, fault codes, inspections, and work-order connections.
- CTA: share sample maintenance records and telematics exports.

Avoid:

- Guaranteed prediction accuracy, guaranteed ROI, "prevents breakdowns", "predicts every failure", "works immediately with any fleet", replaces mechanics, or safety-certified prediction.

## Cross-Product Rules

- Every numerical claim must be sourced, clearly illustrative, or removed.
- Proof boundaries must state what is not being claimed yet.
- Do not include internal pricing, BOM, procurement, credentials, or private operating details.
- If a product is a demo, audit, data-readiness review, or pilot, do not make it sound like mature SaaS.
